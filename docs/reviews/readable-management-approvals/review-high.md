10 findings, most severe first:

1. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:35`: The heading `APPROVAL: Task management` starts the same way as native approve-bead cards (`APPROVAL: design` / `APPROVAL: execution`, where thumbs-up means approve). On this card, though, every approve-type answer selects B, 'leave unchanged'. The ADR only mentions thumbs-up and thumbs-down, and its heading drops the proposal's '(operation consent)' qualifier.

   Failure scenario: In asks.map_reply, the words approve, approved, yes, y, ok, okay and lgtm all map to the recommended choice B, and verbs.APPROVE_EMOJIS (thumbs-up, check mark, hearts) also map to B. Suppose Liam sees 'APPROVAL: Task management' and replies 'approved' or reacts with a check mark, as he does on real approval cards. That records B, the opposite of what he meant. In format2, verify_binding then raises authority_contrary_answer, which permanently holds the request. A later 'A' cannot recover it. The head line also still reads '❓ Ask … · question ·', so the card contradicts its own label. Fix: use a heading that does not start with APPROVAL (or keep the operation-consent qualifier), list every word and emoji that maps to B or none, and add acceptance tests for 'approved', ✅ and ❌.

2. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:41`: 'Preserve existing answer semantics … thumbs-up selects recommended B' is wrong for the legacy format1 path. Legacy Management posts through Handoff.post, which calls supervise.post with its default recommended='A' and the choice 'approve this exact management change'. So on legacy cards, thumbs-up means A and grants the change. Legacy check_ask also binds by title only, against policy approver names.

   Failure scenario: The ADR says both renderers use B-recommended, versioned, source-owned binding (lines 116-121). Applied to format1, that either silently reverses what thumbs-up does there, or puts an APPROVAL heading on a card authorized by matching the title alone. Adding the presentation to that path also means editing bin/btq or btq-supervise (supervise.post hardcodes `--kind question` and recommended A). D4 §279 requires the retained btq and btq-supervise hashes to stay at baseline. The ADR needs to decide explicitly: either format1 is frozen (no new-style posts) or its semantics change is disclosed.

3. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:56`: 'Use existing screening and literal quoting for untrusted text' points at btq code/quote → full → supervise.clean. That is the same `_UNSAFE` `?` substitution that causes the bug, plus `<long>` replacement for 20+ alphanumeric runs and a '(sanitized)' suffix. bin/btq also labels this quoting as a PLACEHOLDER for the unapproved yzm5c design.

   Failure scenario: A human hold such as `Hold: don't reopen until "auth" fix — see btq-x; owner=liam` would show up as `don?t reopen until ?auth? fix ? see btq-x? owner?liam (sanitized)`. A 40-hex SHA or a long branch name becomes `<long>`. The ADR's own sample uses `;`, which is outside the allow-list. D4 §151/§196 require the complete literal hold. The ADR does not say whether such text is mangled or refused, so implementers will reproduce the corruption on material facts. Fix: name the screening (a redact-equality check plus control/bidi rejection) and require refusal whenever a material literal cannot be shown unchanged.

4. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:59`: The required card contents leave out facts D4 says are decisive. Under D4 §57/§166/§179, an A answer also attests that the known review/protection sources are complete and that the work is non-gate. The format2 renderer currently prints 'A attests complete known review/protection sources…' and 'no model-budget reset or worker resumption'. The ADR's contents list and sample drop the attestation, task kind/class and the remaining review budget, all of which D4 §196 requires.

   Failure scenario: A principal answers A on the new readable card without being told that A is also an attestation that no protection record was omitted. That attestation is what lets D4 establish ordinary_work, so it becomes an uninformed attestation. The sample's 'Review: 1 issued, 1 complete' has no remaining budget and no class line, so a card can pass the ADR's golden tests while failing D4's self-contained-card rule.

5. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:75`: The ADR makes the queue responsible for preflighting the 'actual final native parts' (line 127), but the queue cannot compute them. They depend on the ask ID the daemon generates, the daemon's chunk_chars setting, chunk.pack part markers, and redact() applied after rendering. Meanwhile daemon.post_ask truncates and posts anyway (truncated=True).

   Failure scenario: A queue-side estimate (like format2's current '+8 lines/+300 chars' approximation) passes. The daemon then renders a slightly longer head or closing, splits the card, or redacts part of it, and posts and stores it with truncated=True. binding._expected later refuses it as truncated or redacted. The result is a visible 'APPROVAL' card that can never be authorized. Fix: the daemon refuses any presentation-tagged post, before insert, if it would be truncated, changed by redaction or over 36/3200. The queue should not duplicate the native renderer.

6. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:116`: The format2 renderer only exists on the unmerged, frozen btq/btq-1ier9 branch (Format2Management is not on master). The ADR assigns that renderer to the queue task but forbids editing the frozen candidate, and gives no base or order. Format2 records also use closed key sets with format==2, and binding_snapshot requires envelope version==1. There is no 'retained request version' field yet to select a renderer from.

   Failure scenario: The queue implementer has no permitted base for the format2 change. If 1ier9 ships first, format2 cards go live with JSON bodies, and every record from that period needs v1 reproduction kept forever. Adding a version key to the closed record or render_inputs makes existing records fail closed() unless the ADR defines the field name, the default for records that lack it (v1), and how the history index and format numbering change.

7. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:131`: Rollback has no stated owner, order or precondition for new-style requests that are still open or partly applied. The ADR only says rollback is 'verified offline'.

   Failure scenario: If the product is rolled back, the old daemon ignores the presentation metadata. `!details` and `!asks repeat` then re-render with QUESTION: Multiple Options framing that differs from the posted card, and binding-v1 reproduction returns render-mismatch, so every open new-style request holds indefinitely. If the queue is rolled back, the old format2 closed()/history-index checks reject new-version records and the whole management lane holds. Fix: require draining or cancelling active new-style requests (with owner and command), roll the queue back before the product, and add an acceptance test for each direction.

8. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:38`: Nothing limits who may use the `task-management-consent-v1` presentation. The ADR only forbids selecting it implicitly through title, body or poster. Any same-UID local poster can therefore get the authoritative-looking 'APPROVAL: Task management' heading and fixed A/B framing on arbitrary text.

   Failure scenario: Any local script with ask.sock access posts `--presentation task-management-consent-v1` with a body describing some other action, under a poster name like 'btq-management'. The phone shows a genuine-looking management approval. An operator who answers A has given apparent consent that people and tooling may later treat as meaningful. The ADR should restrict the presentation to the management poster or another bound identity, or at least say on the card that it carries no authority unless a management request binding exists.

9. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:97`: The single answer instruction 'Reply A or B. Thumbs-up selects B; thumbs-down grants nothing.' leaves out the `!answer <id> <text>` route from the native answer_line. It also does not warn that any other answer from a current-policy principal (thumbs-down/none, B, or a free-text note) permanently vetoes or holds the request.

   Failure scenario: On format2, verify_binding treats any authenticated answer whose choice is not A as authority_contrary_answer, and any authenticated note as authority_ambiguous_answer. One reply such as 'A, but check X first' or 'ok' turns the card into a dead request, and a later A cannot revive it. The operator was told only 'grants nothing'. If replies cannot be threaded on the phone, the missing `!answer` instruction also leaves no documented way to answer.

10. `docs/adr/2026-10-19-readable-management-approvals.md:103`: The preferred 'additive side table keyed by ask ID' adds orphan and duplicate states that the ADR then has to refuse. Store already has a pattern for this: additive nullable columns added once, all-or-none, with no backfill (store.py _CXMKF_COLUMNS, refreshed_from), where NULL means historical.

    Failure scenario: A side table needs its own join in Store.ask, binding.stored_inputs and read_snapshot, plus orphan/duplicate detection and its own tests. A nullable `presentation` column inserted in the same row is atomic by construction. On rollback it behaves the same, because the old daemon's fixed `_ASK` INSERT leaves it NULL. The 'positional legacy ask-row writes' concern applies equally to the existing cxmkf columns, which were added safely.
