- **[NON-BLOCKING] Option 2 is acceptable under the stated trust boundary.** **“Residuals,” lines 201–206; “Changes,” line 274.** Removing, reassigning, or adding an allocation requires administrative authority on the supported host, putting residual 4 in residual 2’s privileged-authority class. Routine administration can trigger it without malicious intent, but the design now explicitly accepts that scenario and qualifies its guarantee.

- **[NON-BLOCKING] Describe stability as the effective allocation, including identity resolution.** **Lines 76–87, 161, 201–206, 274.** For example, renaming A while leaving an old name-keyed `/etc/subuid` entry unchanged can make a historical mapping disappear from the scan’s selected ranges. That is still an administrative change, but not necessarily an edit to `/etc/subuid`. Numeric and name entries are otherwise both covered. A configured subid plugin—including a user-controlled backend—fails closed because any `subid:` line selects “all.” I found no ordinary unprivileged escape on the supported conventional configuration. Clarifying the wording is sufficient for this PoC. See [subuid source and entry semantics](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/subuid.5.html).

- **[NON-BLOCKING] The narrower mapping alternative remains worth a follow-up; the reported cost does not measure it.** **“Why not the kernel’s mapping,” lines 89–104.** Keeping only non-identity maps whose outside ranges intersect A or A’s current allocation would exclude, for example, Chrome’s disjoint mapping for another user. The 48–50-process census and approximately 4% sample rate measure the broader rule. However, the narrower rule does **not** close residual 4: an old namespace can map only removed range R, omit A, and then have no overlap with the current allocation. Further nesting can also drop A’s mapping. It is sound under the same stable-allocation premise, not a replacement that establishes the stronger invariant. It need not enter this design. See [mapping visibility and namespace rules](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/user_namespaces.7.html). My bounded local snapshot exposed no foreign processes, so it could not independently quantify its cost.
